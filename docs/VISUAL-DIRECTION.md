# Visual Direction

## Intent

The Grotto is a buried structure that should not exist: ancient architecture fused with industrial remnants and living resonance. The visual slice should feel dark, surreal, subterranean, oppressive, and occasionally beautiful without becoming generic fantasy, sci-fi corridors, or asset-store horror.

## Environment palette

The base palette is near-black blue, charcoal stone, oxidized green, and bruised violet. Warm resonance colors are earned and reserved for authored beats: tarnished gold for Echo artifacts, cyan-teal for safe orientation lights, magenta-violet for the music event, and red-violet for the Warden arena. Avoid rainbow lighting and unmotivated neon.

## Material language

Use a compact vocabulary: matte ancient stone, dark metal, oxidized metal, wet organic growth, void-corrupted surfaces, and emissive resonance glass. Surfaces should be rough and imperfect. Emissive elements are small, intentional accents rather than whole-room color washes.

## Lighting language

Every space has one readable path light and deep shadow beyond it. The Grotto is cool and stable; the descent narrows and becomes more violet; the music chamber wakes with controlled magenta light; the Warden arena uses a restrained red-violet pulse. Fog provides depth and scale but must not hide traversal or silhouettes.

## Architecture language

Arches, ribs, buried columns, broken slabs, embedded machinery, and asymmetrical frames suggest a structure that was built, abandoned, and then grown through. Repetition should feel modular but not sterile. Use strong vertical silhouettes and deliberate negative space around gameplay landmarks.

## Creature language

The Hollow Echo is a compact, low silhouette with a dark rose core. The Resonant Warden is not a larger demon or robot: it is an architectural guardian with a tall offset mineral mass, unequal shoulders, broken buttresses, a crown-like structural break, and a recessed resonance mechanism protected by interrupted ribs. Its red-violet activity comes from the integrated mechanism and damaged seams, not from a glowing humanoid body.

## VFX language

Effects are sparse and communicative: drifting dust for atmosphere, a small resonance particle field for the music event, a contained halo around the Echo, hit flashes for combat readability, and a restrained activation/defeat pulse for the Warden.

## UI language

Typography is minimal, uppercase, and spacious. Use pale mineral text, teal orientation labels, ochre interaction prompts, and red-violet threat messaging. Keep the HUD quiet so the environment carries the identity.

## References

Reference qualities include subterranean industrial ruins, eroded monolithic architecture, bioluminescent mineral growth, analog machinery, and abstract music visualization. These are mood references only; no external visual asset is imported in this pass.

## Things to avoid

Avoid medieval fantasy props, clean spaceship corridors, excessive neon, rainbow grading, asset-pack soup, noisy decals, photorealistic clutter, unmotivated fog, and visual effects that obscure the player or enemy silhouette.

## Production asset refinement

The first production hero asset is the Echo. It should read as an ancient object that has been corrupted and reactivated by music: a splintered dark mineral mass bound by a broken oxidized-metal frame, leaking restrained violet resonance through its fractures.

### Echo construction language

- **Core**: a convex-hull mineral mass built from a handful of widely spaced points, so the form carries a few large planar facets instead of a rounded shell. It is deliberately asymmetric, with cut fracture pockets that expose resonance from inside and shallow dark seams across the broadest facets.
- **Frame**: hand-authored claw straps swept along curved paths, a slipped diagonal strap, a back spine, and a detached remnant of a former hoop with jagged snapped ends. Thickness varies along every strap, arcs are interrupted rather than closed, and several straps intersect and cradle the core. Nothing encircles the core as a clean ring.
- **Shards**: tapered resonance blades that grow out of the frame junctions at varied sizes and orientations. The blade body stays dark mineral; only the outer section carries resonance, so emission reads as flow out of the artifact.

The bright material stays small and earned. The frame remains dark and legible, the mineral carries the mass, and tarnished gold appears only as junction hardware. This preserves the established gold/purple artifact role while making the Echo readable as a singular designed object rather than a primitive plus loose decoration.

### Silhouette rule

The Echo must read as a unique object before materials and emission are visible. If its outline can be described as "crystal plus ring", the construction is wrong regardless of surface treatment. Shape, silhouette, construction, material relationship, and asymmetry are the primary tools; emission is used only after those are working.
